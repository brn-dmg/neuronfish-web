## What Is Dikkha AI?

Dikkha AI is a free study assistant app for Class 9–10 students in Bangladesh, available on Android. It lives inside the student's textbook — instead of searching Google or asking a generic chatbot, a student simply opens their NCTB book inside the app, highlights a confusing line, and instantly gets help in Bangla.

It is not a tutoring platform. It is not a chat app. It is a **reading companion** — built around the exact moment a student gets stuck while studying.

---

## The Problem It Solves

Class 9–10 is a make-or-break academic phase in Bangladesh. Students face:

- Dense NCTB textbooks with no interactive layer
- No affordable 24/7 tutoring access outside of coaching centres
- Generic AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini) that don't know the Bangladesh curriculum and answer in English
- Zero structured revision tools — flashcards, formula sheets, exam suggestions are all scattered

Dikkha AI collapses all of this into one free app, purpose-built for this student.

## Core Features

### 1. AI Explain
The flagship feature. Students highlight any text in their NCTB textbook and tap "Explain this." The AI reads the surrounding context, understands what the student is asking, and gives a clear, student-friendly answer **in Bangla** — including mathematical steps if the topic needs it.

> **Why it matters:** This is the "stuck moment" feature. It removes the need to call a friend, ask a tutor, or wade through YouTube.

---

### 2. Flashcard Generator
From any highlighted passage, students can generate flashcards instantly for active recall practice. No manual card creation. No typing. The AI builds the card from what the student just read.

> **Why it matters:** Flashcard-based revision is one of the most evidence-backed study methods. Dikkha automates it.

---

### 3. Formula Sheet
A searchable, AI-explained formula library covering every formula across the Class 9–10 curriculum. Students can look up a formula and get a plain-language explanation of what it means and when to use it.

> **Why it matters:** Formula confusion is one of the top pain points in Science and Math subjects. This is a single-destination fix.

---

### 4. AI Infographics
Students can generate visual concept summaries — AI-created infographics — for topics they find hard to retain. Complex ideas become visual memory anchors.

> **Why it matters:** Visual learning is especially effective for diagram-heavy subjects like Biology, Geography, and Physics.

---

### 5. SSC 2026 Exam Suggestions
A dedicated section inside the app mapped directly to NCTB chapters, giving students:
- Subject-wise suggestion lists for SSC 2026
- High-importance topics per chapter, clearly marked
- Question type breakdown (MCQ, short answer, broad questions)
- Built-in exam countdown

> **Why it matters:** Exam prep is the highest-urgency moment for this student segment. This feature turns the app from a "nice to have" into an essential tool in the months before SSC.

---

### 6. Full Subject Library (16+ Textbooks)
All NCTB Class 9–10 textbooks across Science, Commerce, and Arts groups are available inside the app. Students don't need physical books to use Dikkha — the entire curriculum is in one place.

> **Why it matters:** Penetration beyond students who already have physical books. Useful for students in underserved areas with limited access to materials.

# Marketing Angles to Explore

---

## Angle 1 — "রাত ১১টায় কে বোঝাবে?" (Who will explain at 11pm?)

The most honest angle. Every student in Bangladesh has had this moment: it's the night before an exam, something in the chapter isn't clicking, and there's no one to call. Their tutor is unavailable. Their parents can't help. Their brilliant friend is also studying.

Dikkha AI is that friend — available at any hour, explains in Bangla, never gets tired of questions.

**Why it works:** This is a felt experience that every student recognises instantly. It's emotional, not feature-based. The campaign writes itself in social video format — a student alone at night with a textbook, phone in hand. Show the moment, then show the solution.

**Channels:** Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts, class WhatsApp groups. High shareability because it names a universal experience.

---

## Angle 2 — "এখনই নাও, বিনামূল্যে" (Get it now, while it's free)

Dikkha AI is currently in beta — free for the next 3 months. That window closes. This is a scarcity-and-generosity angle: students who find it now get full AI tutoring at zero cost, before pricing begins.

The framing isn't "free forever" — it's "you got in early." That's a status hook for students. Sharing the app becomes an act of helping friends: "ভাই এটা এখন ফ্রি, পরে হয়তো টাকা লাগবে — নামিয়ে নে।" ("Bro this is free right now, might cost later — download it.") Peer-to-peer spread through WhatsApp and Messenger happens organically when there's a time element attached.

**Why it works:** "Free" alone doesn't move people in Bangladesh — they assume there's a catch. But "free *right now*, not forever" creates urgency and removes skepticism. It also sets up a paid tier narrative for later without making it feel like a bait-and-switch, because you were upfront from day one.

**Channels:** WhatsApp class groups, Facebook student communities, Telegram study channels. Short, forwardable message format works best — students relay it themselves.

---

## Angle 3 — SSC 2026: "শেষ সুযোগ নষ্ট করো না"

SSC is not just an exam in Bangladesh — it's a cultural moment. Students, parents, extended family all carry the weight of it. The exam determines college admission, family honour, and in many cases, the student's own self-worth.

This is a time-bound, urgency-driven campaign built entirely around SSC 2026. The suggestion feature — chapter-wise important topics, question type breakdowns, exam countdown — is the entry point. But the broader message is: **the students who prepare smart, not just hard, are the ones who score.**

**Why it works:** Exam season dramatically spikes app downloads and engagement for every edtech product in Bangladesh. This is the moment to acquire users while intent is highest. The campaign should feel like it's on the student's side — a preparation guide, not a product ad.

**Channels:** SSC 2026 Facebook groups (300K+ members), YouTube channels of popular teachers, student-run Telegram and WhatsApp broadcast lists. These communities actively share preparation resources.

---

## Angle 4 — "যে বোঝে, বোঝায়" (Who understands, explains)

The wordplay is the point — "বোঝে" (understands) and "বোঝায়" (explains) in the same breath. It describes the ideal study companion: someone who actually gets the material and can break it down for you. Not a teacher. Not a formal tutor. Something closer — something that gets you.

Dikkha reads the same textbook. Explains in the same Bangla. Doesn't make you feel stupid for asking the same thing three times. The late-night question that feels too embarrassing to send your teacher — you can ask Dikkha.

**Why it works:** It's an identity-based angle — not "use this tool" but "have this companion." The catchphrase is short, rhythmic, very Bengali, and memorable without being cringe. It reframes the product from an app into a relationship, which is a much stronger emotional hook than any feature list.

**Channels:** Student influencer collaborations — Class 9–10 students with modest but highly engaged followings (10K–100K) on Facebook and YouTube convert far better than celebrity endorsements for this audience. Authentic, peer-to-peer tone only.

---

## Angle 5 — "নিজেই বোঝো, মুখস্থ না করে" (Understand it yourself, don't just memorise)

Bangladesh's education system is heavily rote-learning driven. Students memorise answers without understanding them — and everyone, including students and parents, knows this is a problem. There's cultural shame around "শুধু মুখস্থ করা" (just memorising).

Dikkha AI offers actual understanding — the AI explains *why* something works, not just *what* to write in the exam. The flashcard system reinforces active recall, not passive reading.

**Why it works:** It taps into an existing cultural tension. Students want to understand; the system forces them to memorise. Dikkha is on their side against that system. This angle also appeals to the parents who worry their child is cramming without comprehension.

**Channels:** Content marketing through educational YouTube and Facebook pages. Long-form video essays ("why our students memorise but don't understand — and what to do about it") build trust and pull users in organically. Teachers and tutors sharing this angle adds credibility.

## The Technology (Plain English)

The AI underneath Dikkha is not a basic chatbot. It uses a method called **Agentic RAG** — which means:

1. When a student highlights text, the app understands the *intent* behind what they selected (not just the literal words)
2. It rewrites the query to be more precise before searching for answers
3. It only retrieves answers from curriculum-aligned sources — not random internet content
4. The answer it generates is grounded in what the student's textbook actually says

The result: answers that feel like a knowledgeable teacher responded, not a search engine.
